FoodHub Market

"FoodHub Market" is a big food market with many food stalls (small restaurants) under one roof. You are requested to design and develop a system for managing the stalls, customer orders, and food pickup.

For each stall, the system stores a unique number, name, food types (e.g., pizza, sushi, burgers, etc.), and location in the market – floor number and position (x,y in the floor). Only the market manager can manage these details.

Stall representatives are responsible for managing the dishes of their stalls. Each dish has a name, food type, image, and ingredients. The list of ingredients (including ID and name) is automatically generated from the dish image using vision algorithms like YOLO or VLM. The stall representative may confirm or edit the generated ingredients list.

The Ministry of Health periodically provides an official catalog of allergens and the ingredients associated with them in XML format. The system imports the catalog and updates its information accordingly. For each allergen, the system stores its code, name, and description. This information is used to automatically generate allergen warnings and declarations that will be shown when a customer orders a dish that contains those ingredients.

Customers can order dishes using the FoodHub app. When placing an order, the customer provides their phone number. If the customer does not exist, the system asks for details to register the customer: first name, last name, phone number, and email. The customer is then free to select dishes from different stalls in the same order. For each ordered dish, the system displays the dish details and the customer is requested to set the ordered quantity.

Besides individual orders, customers may place group orders. A group order is created by one customer, who becomes the order owner. When created, the owner may specify purpose (in text, for example: team lunch, birthday celebration, family gathering). The owner then invites other customers to join the order using a unique group code (text) or using a unique QR code (as an image). If any of the customers do not already exist at the time of joining the group, they will register then. While the order is in status of opened, Each customer can add, modify, or remove only their own dishes until the order owner finalizes and submits the order, then the status will change to ordered. Once the order is submitted, it is processed like individual orders.

Staff members (including the market manager and the stall representatives) can update the status of each order throughout its lifecycle from the point that it has already been ordered, i.e., being cooked, ready to be served or served. 

The customers can also see the bill through the app. For group orders, each customer sees his/ her part of the bill (the price of the dishes he/ she ordered). The payment is handled outside the system by a dedicated PayMe system. The "FoodHub Market" system sends PayMe the customer's details and the amount to pay. When PayMe confirms the payment, the "FoodHub Market" system records the payment allocation per stall, enabling market management to track exactly how much revenue was received for each stall. The "FoodHub Market" system emails a receipt with the customer's details, the items ordered, the price of each item, and the total amount.

The system creates these reports:

1. **Yearly Revenue Report ** – For each year, it shows the total revenue generated by each stall in the market. In addition to the total revenue, the report displays the percentage of the stall’s revenue out of the market’s overall revenue for the same year, allowing the market manager to evaluate each stall’s contribution to the market’s financial performance. This report is shown as a graph of the trends between the years. Ordered by year (ascending). The market manager will get this report in PDF format, while a JSON version will be generated for the “Mayerowicz & Mendelbaum“ Accounting Firm for tax purposes.

2. ** New vs. Returning Customers Report ** – A monthly report comparing the number of first-time customers to returning customers for a month given as a parameter. When generated by a stall representative, the report shows for that stall the number of first-time customers (customers whose very first order ever was placed at that stall during the given month) and the number of returning customers (customers who had placed at least one order at the stall before that month, and ordered again during the given month). The report also calculates the percentage of returning customers out of all customers who ordered in that month. When generated by the market manager, the information will be market-wide. Both versions of the report  are ordered by the descending percentage of returning customers,  generated in PDF format and can optionally be compared side-by-side with the previous month's figures to show differences.

Additional Story
A group order begins when one customer, who becomes the group owner, chooses to create it. The system generates a unique group code and QR code, which the owner can then share with other customers so they can join the order (the maximal number of participants in a group order is 5, including the owner). Once a participant joins, they can add dishes from one or several stalls, as well as update or remove their own ordered dishes. Modifications can be made until the order is submitted, and independently of other participants. 

Only the owner can submit the group order. Once the owner succeeds in submitting the order (which is possible only after all participants have paid for their share), no participant, including the owner, can add, modify, or remove any ordered dish. The system then sends the ordered dishes to the right stalls and informs the owner that the order is in preparation. The participants can further follow the progress of each of their ordered dishes, as the stall’s staff members report online when they start and finish preparing each ordered dish.

Dish preparation may take up to 30 minutes (from the submission). Any dish that is not ready by then is canceled and fully refunded to the participant who ordered it.

If a stall temporarily stops its kitchen operations (e.g., due to a short equipment issue), all of its ordered dishes that are not yet ready are paused. Once the stall resumes operation, each dish continues from where it was before the pause, whether or not its cooking had already started. The 30-minute limit keeps running during a pause, so a dish that is still paused when the limit expires is canceled and refunded like any other dish that is not ready. If a stall remains paused for more than 10 minutes, all of its dishes that are not yet ready are canceled and fully refunded.

When all uncanceled dishes in a group order are ready, a waiter is called. Upon the waiter’s arrival, the ordered dishes are served, and the order is closed. If all dishes in an order are canceled, the order is canceled.
